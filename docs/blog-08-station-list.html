A Grouped Station List and a Personal Watch List

The station picker now groups every station by state, and a new Watch button lets you build your own shortcut list instead of a fixed set of seven.

The Blast Radius · 23 August 2026
← The Blast Radius ← Range Rings, Long-Range Accuracy, and the Echo Inspector Open RadEcho →

Grouped by state, sorted by city

The station dropdown used to list every station in raw ICAO-code order — readable once you know the codes, opaque otherwise. It's now grouped by state, states in alphabetical order, and stations sorted by city name within each state. Looking for the nearest station to a specific place is now a matter of scanning one state group instead of scrolling a flat list of codes.

A couple of stations don't fit a U.S. state: overseas military sites (Kunsan, Camp Humphreys) get their own "Overseas" group, and the handful of TDWR terminal-radar codes that have no city/state on record at all (present in the data but without a name attached) are grouped together as "Other" at the end, instead of showing up as unlabeled codes scattered through the middle of the list.

A personal Watch list

The shortcut row at the top of the dropdown — previously a fixed set of seven stations — is now yours to set. A new ☆ Watch button next to the station picker adds or removes the current station from your own list; once added, it turns into ★ Watched and the station shows up in that top shortcut row every time you open the picker.

Useful if the stations you check regularly aren't the ones RadEcho happened to ship with by default — build the list around wherever you actually live, work, or watch weather for. It's saved in your browser, so it persists across visits without needing an account.
