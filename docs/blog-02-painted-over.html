Painted Over

The labelled view fixed deletion by hiding it under solid color. That turned out to be the same failure wearing a different color — and a clear, calm night is what proved it. What replaced it, and why nothing in the default view touches a pixel's true value anymore.

The Blast Radius · 12 August 2026
← The Blast Radius ← Holes In The Storms Open RadEcho →

Where the last post left off

Holes In The Storms ended on what felt like the right answer: stop deleting gates on a ρHV threshold, because no threshold can tell heavy rain from clutter apart, and label instead — paint non-weather gates slate grey (biological) or earthy brown (clutter/AP) and leave everything else showing its true reflectivity. The holes closed. Nothing was destroyed anymore. That shipped as the default four days before this post and it held for exactly four days.

It didn't fail because the classifier was wrong. It failed because painting a gate solid grey or solid brown is still overwriting its true value — and that turns out to matter in two directions at once, both discovered the same week.

A clear, calm night turns the screen brown

On the night of 2026-08-09/10 — clear sky, light wind, no weather anywhere — the labelled default filled with grey and brown at four stations independently. Measured, with METARs swept across each station's full coverage circle at scan time:

The classifier was not wrong. Every one of those brown and grey regions really was non-weather — KBUF is the control, with genuine precipitation in its western coverage, and its non-weather fraction is correspondingly half the other three. The problem was that correct and useless can be the same thing. A screen that's 80–87% grey and brown doesn't read as "here's what's not real weather." It reads as noise.

Most of it wasn't terrain — it was the atmosphere lying that night specifically

Terrain returns are the same targets on every scan. Anomalous propagation is a propagation state — whether the atmosphere is bending the beam into ground it normally clears. Same station, same day, midday versus the same clear calm night: 

KENX  midday (16:00Z):  5,643 clutter gates,  2,671 cells
KENX  night  (02:00Z): 74,835 clutter gates, 14,845 cells
      cells in both:     2,329   <- the genuine fixed-terrain core
      night-only cells: 12,516   <- 84.3% of that night's footprint

KRAX  midday: 12,458 / 5,761   ->  night 79,857 / 19,972   (75.2% night-only)
KBUF  midday: 19,780 / 5,630   ->  night 68,709 / 12,673   (61.0% night-only)

A 13x jump in clutter gates at the same radar, looking at the same unchanged hills, on the same day. This is a nocturnal radiation inversion — clear sky, calm wind, the ground radiating heat away and bending the beam down into terrain it normally overshoots. And there's a real physical coupling behind why this always shows up worst exactly when the labelled view has nothing to protect: clear-air scan patterns (VCP 31/34/35) are selected because there's no precipitation, and they deliberately censor at a much lower SNR threshold specifically to reveal faint returns — which is also exactly the regime where radiation inversions and AP peak. The entire argument for label-don't-delete (real weather should never be erased) is inactive on a night when there's no real weather within 230 km, and that's precisely the night the display is at its worst.

The tempting fix, tried and reverted the same night

The obvious response: default to the filtered view on clear-air VCPs (nothing to protect) and the labelled view on precipitation VCPs (where deletion is dangerous). As a cheaper stand-in, a plain dBZ floor was tried as the clear-air default instead of resurrecting the RhoHV filter — hide everything below some intensity, no quality signal involved, nothing that could mistake a hailstone for a hillside.

It went out, and within hours came back reported as "moth holed again" on KRAX — a real rain band northwest of Durham. Measured on the actual volume: 

KRAX20260810_022135_V06, VCP 212, 02:24:40Z — rain band NW of Durham

gates >=5 dBZ in the band:  11,171
rendered (passed the old RhoHV cut):  3,679   (32.9%)
DROPPED:                               7,492   (67.1%)   median 9.5 dBZ, 61.4 km

why the CMD rescue pass didn't save them:
  below the near-field rescue floor:  7,432   (99.2% of the drops)
  scored clutter on CMD:                 42   (0.6%)
  ZDR at/above the biological ceiling:   18   (0.2%)

Two-thirds of a real rain band, gone. Light rain at 61 km is weak — median 9.5 dBZ — and weak echo means low signal-to-noise, and low SNR biases ρHV downward independent of what's actually there. So genuine light precipitation fails a correlation cut it has no business failing, and the rescue pass's own near-field floor (tuned to keep it from rescuing genuine clutter) excludes that exact population from being saved. The floor and the RhoHV filter are different mechanisms hiding a similar amount of data, but they fail in opposite shapes: the filter punches holes through a solid band, the floor recedes a clean edge. Both hide roughly the same weather; only one of them looks broken doing it. That's a real distinction and it isn't nothing — but hiding two-thirds of a real rain band is still hiding two-thirds of a real rain band, on any device that reaches for the label "default."

The stopgap was reverted the same night, undeployed. Production stayed on the labelled default while the real fix got designed.

The actual fix: stop painting, period

Here's the piece that took longest to see. The labelled view's grey and brown fills weren't just cosmetically loud on a clear night — they were structurally the same category of mistake as the black holes from the first post, just wearing a friendlier color:

Replacing a gate's true reflectivity with a flat grey or brown erases the gradient underneath it. A weak insect bloom and a dense one paint identically. And on a misclassified gate inside a genuine severe storm — a hail core with naturally low ρHV, exactly the case CMD is prone to mistag at long range — solid brown doesn't just mislabel the gate. It masks a real 50–70 dBZ echo and shows the user a void where a dangerous storm actually is. That is the black hole from the first post, recolored.

So the fix wasn't a better threshold, a VCP-aware default, or a smarter floor. It was retiring the entire delete-on-quality-signal architecture — not just leaving it opt-in, removing it — and separating classification from the pixel it classifies entirely.

What actually got deleted (the code, this time, on purpose)

The RhoHV delete branches in both rasterizers, the despeckle/suppress/rescue pipeline that existed only to make deletion survivable, the "Hide low-confidence returns" checkbox, and `filterRhoHV`'s place in both cache keys — gone. None of that machinery is needed once nothing deletes on a quality signal anymore. The classifier itself (`isClutterGate`, CMD, the ZDR biological ceiling) was untouched; it still computes exactly the same call on exactly the same moments. What changed is what happens to that call's answer.

What ships now

Classification became a second, independent, fully transparent raster layer — rendered separately, cached separately, composited in the browser on top of the base reflectivity rather than baked into it: 

Basemap (dark / RadBlast)
  1. .radar-frame     — true dBZ reflectivity, 100% unmodified, always on
  2. .overlay-frame    — transparent tags, opt-in, off by default
  3. .labels           — roads / place names, always on top
Rendered asWhat it meansCondition
Cyan (60% alpha)Biological — insects, birdsZDR ≥ 3.0 dB
Amber (60% alpha)Ground clutter / APρHV < 0.95, CMD ≥ 0.25, beam height < 2 km
Fully transparentEverything elseTrue dBZ ramp shows through, untouched

The toggle is Non-weather tags, and it ships unchecked. When it's off, the client never even requests the overlay tiles — zero network cost, not just zero visual effect. When it's on, the true color underneath a tagged gate is still fully visible through the tint; a 60 dBZ core that gets tagged amber reads as translucent amber over red, not solid brown over nothing. Base reflectivity tiles are byte-identical whether the toggle is on or off — verified directly, not assumed, because that's the whole argument for why this was safe to ship.

Nothing in the default view can delete a gate or replace its color anymore. Not a threshold someone forgot to check, not a floor tuned wrong for one intensity band — there's no mechanism left in the default path capable of either. The base raster is, byte for byte, the Level II moment the radar actually measured.

One real bug surfaced building this, worth keeping because of what it says about testing a layered system: the overlay silently failed to render at all over a real clutter cluster near Fredonia — traced to the worker pool never receiving the overlay's destination file path, so it just skipped generating it, and the cache-validity check only ever looked for the base raster file, so it happily served a permanently-missing overlay as if nothing were wrong. A two-layer system needs both layers checked, not just the one users complain about first.

What's still true from the first post, and what's now wrong in it

Holes In The Storms remains the right explanation of the physics — why a ρHV threshold can never cleanly separate clutter from real weather, why CMD's texture axis works where a correlation cut doesn't, why the radar's own upstream clutter filter can subtract but nothing downstream can. None of that changed. What changed is the last section of it: the "current build" it describes — solid grey and brown baked into the default view — is no longer what ships. That's this post.

Carried forward unchanged, because nothing here touched them:

  • The Arkwright wind farm still renders as a 50 dBZ storm, untagged even with the overlay on. Turbine blades move, so they sail through the radar's own upstream notch filter same as before; they read ρHV 0.985, which clears the 0.95 cut comfortably, so the classifier never flags them amber. Same open problem, same root cause, unaffected by any of this.
  • Deep convection at extreme range can still score raggedness on CMD and get tagged amber inside a genuine storm core — the height guard above 3 km ARL catches most of it, not all.
  • The colors are still provisional. Cyan and amber were picked to be visually distinct from the dBZ ramp and legible over the dark basemap; the terrain-basemap legibility pass never happened.
