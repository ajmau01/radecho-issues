Range Rings, Long-Range Accuracy, and the Echo Inspector

Three smaller additions worth a proper writeup: distance rings from the crosshair with a km/mi preference, corrected long-range positioning beyond 160 km, and a way to read raw values off any point on the radar.

The Blast Radius · 23 August 2026

Range rings and a real unit preference

Show Range Rings, in the legend, draws a set of distance rings out from the crosshair — six rings at round distances (25, 50, 100, 150, 200, and 250 km, or 10, 25, 50, 75, 100, and 150 mi depending on your unit setting), each labeled with its distance. Useful for judging at a glance how far a storm actually is from a specific point, rather than estimating against the basemap alone.

Alongside the rings, RadEcho now has a real app-wide km / mi preference (in the Display section of the toolbar) — the first time this app has had a persisted unit setting. It's not just the ring labels: switching units changes every distance shown in the app, including the Echo Inspector's range readout and the nearest-station distance shown by Locate. Switching units also swaps in a genuinely different, unit-appropriate set of ring radii rather than just relabeling the same physical circles — the mi-mode rings aren't metric ones with a converted label.

The rings themselves are drawn using the app's real spherical geodesy, the same math that positions the radar data itself (see the long-range accuracy fix below) — not a flat circle, which would visibly bow away from a true distance ring at longer radii and higher latitudes.

Range rings ship off by default, same as every non-radar overlay.

Corrected long-range positioning

Radar gates beyond about 160 km from the station were being positioned using an approximation that treats the Earth as flat near the station — accurate enough close in, but measurably wrong at long range and high latitude. That's now replaced with true spherical geodesy everywhere in the app, not just close to the radar. In practice this means far-range echoes — a storm 200+ km out, right at the edge of a station's useful coverage — now sit at their true position on the map, rather than being subtly displaced.

This is a pure accuracy fix with no new controls to turn on — it's just correct now, for every station, at every range, everywhere gate positions get computed: the base render, the non-weather shading overlay, and the new range rings above.

Echo Inspector: raw values at any point

Click Inspect, then click or tap any point on the radar, and a popup reports the actual values driving what's rendered there: reflectivity in dBZ, range and azimuth from the station, ZDR and RhoHV (the dual-polarization measurements RadEcho's own clutter and biological-scatter filtering is based on), a CMD texture score, and the resulting classification. Useful for understanding exactly why a given pixel looks the way it does, rather than taking the rendered color on faith.

Echo Inspector requires Native style — gate-level data isn't available in the classic grid-rendering style, so the Inspect button is disabled there. It reports the same values regardless of whether smoothing is on, and flags a value as smoothed rather than raw where an older cached scan predates raw-value capture, instead of silently mixing the two.

This is the first phase of a planned two-part feature — beam height/altitude at the clicked point is intentionally not included yet, held back until the underlying elevation-angle groundwork it depends on is further along.
