What's New in RadEcho

A running log of updates to the radar viewer — written for people using it, not just the changelog.

August 3, 2026

Zoom no longer drifts away from the location you searched for

A tester centered the map on their house and found that zooming — especially pinching, the way almost everyone zooms on a phone — would gradually carry the view somewhere else entirely. Fixed, along with the button that was supposed to get you back.

🎯
Zoom now stays anchored to your search Pinching, the +/- buttons, mouse wheel, and a quick two-finger tap all zoom in and out on the location you actually searched for — they can no longer walk the view away from it, no matter how many times you zoom in a row.
↩️
"Reset view" actually resets the view now It used to either keep whatever drift had already happened or jump to the radar station's own location instead of yours. It now genuinely returns you to the exact spot you searched for.
👆
Still want to look somewhere else? Double-click or double-tap. That's unchanged — a deliberate double-click (or double-tap on mobile) still zooms in on exactly wherever you clicked, for exploring a different area without needing to search a new location first.

The zoom buttons also got a small crosshair-styled icon, as a visual reminder of what they're anchored to.

August 3, 2026

A clearer way to pick how many frames

Small polish: the Frames field is now a dropdown instead of a free-typing number box.

📋
Frames is now a preset dropdown Previously you could type any number, but anything past 20 silently got capped without telling you. Now it's a straightforward dropdown — 3, 6, 10, 15, or 20 — so the actual range is obvious instead of something you'd only discover by hitting it.

August 2, 2026

Auto-refresh: the radar now updates on its own

A tester asked for this directly: leave RadEcho open on a station and it'll pick up new scans by itself, no manual reload needed.

↻
New "Auto-refresh" checkbox Check it after loading a station and RadEcho quietly checks for a new scan about once a minute. When one lands, it just appears — if you're already looking at the latest frame, the view advances to the new one automatically; if you've scrubbed back to review an earlier frame, it leaves you right where you are and just makes the new frame available.
Frames Style

The Display row in the toolbar — new checkbox on the right.

⚡
Faster behind the scenes The server now keeps actively-watched stations' data warmed up in the background, so both auto-refresh and a plain manual reload come back much faster than before for anything you've looked at recently.
🧩
Loads appear all at once now, not tile by tile Previously a load could visibly pop in a piece at a time over several seconds, even though the server had already finished — that was purely how the pieces were being fetched, roughly 150 separate image requests per load. Now it's about 7. The picture appears as one unit; auto-refresh's own updates benefit from the same fix.
🎞️
Longer animation loops The Frames field now goes up to 20 (was 10) — loop back through close to two hours of history instead of about 50 minutes, useful for actually watching how a system's moving rather than just its last few scans.
Frames (was capped at 10)
🔍
Deeper zoom Zoom now goes noticeably further in — enough to frame in on your own neighborhood instead of just the wider metro area. Worth knowing: past a certain point you're not getting sharper storm detail (NEXRAD's own resolution tops out around 250m per pixel, same as always) — you're getting a tighter, closer-in view of the same data, which genuinely helps when something's bearing down on a specific spot.
🎯
A real favicon The browser tab has an actual icon now instead of a blank page — a small radar sweep, dark to match the app.
